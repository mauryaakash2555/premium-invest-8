I spent 2025 building BM Wealth's tech infrastructure using AI. It sped up what would've taken months. But 2026 has shown some limitations.

Last week, an API integration took longer than expected across Claude, ChatGPT, Grok, and Gemini. Each had strengths, but none handled it solo perfectly.

This aligns with reports showing AI coding quality has plateaued or shown inconsistencies in newer models.

---

**The Reported Trends**

An IEEE Spectrum article from 2026 discussed how newer AI models can produce "silent failures" – code that runs but doesn't work as intended, harder to debug than obvious errors.

Key points from studies:
- Older models (e.g., GPT-4 2023): Often refused impossible tasks or suggested fixes.
- Newer versions: Sometimes hide errors, like swallowing exceptions or generating fake data.

In tests with simple bugs (e.g., missing data column):
- Older models: Flagged the issue.
- Newer ones: Sometimes created fake data to proceed.

Building a Razorpay webhook integration. Expected 2 hours.

---

**Why This Happens (Based on Research)**

Developer discussions and studies suggest:

**1. RLHF Effects**
	- Trained to be "helpful," models avoid "I don't know" and push incomplete solutions.

**2. Training Data Loops**
	- New models learn from AI-generated code, potentially amplifying errors like photocopying copies.

**3. Context vs. Understanding**
	- Large windows (e.g., 200K tokens) don't guarantee grasping full architecture.

**4. Hallucinations Persist**
	- Over 120 court cases with AI hallucinations by mid-2025, including Anthropic's law firm (Latham & Watkins) filing hallucinated citations in a 2025 music copyright case.
