I've prepared GST returns for restaurants in Mumbai since 2017. In the last few years, I've seen several clients struggle or even close due to compliance pressures—not food quality or location.
Let me share the real numbers based on current 2026 rules.

The GST Promise vs 2026 Reality
What they said in 2017: "Simpler tax, easier compliance, good for small business"
What happens to a small Mumbai restaurant today:
Typical Client Profile:

Location: Malad/Goregaon
Type: 60-seat non-AC standalone restaurant
Annual turnover: ₹80 lakhs
Profit margin: 8-12% (industry standard)


Pre-GST Era (VAT System - 2016):
Tax & Compliance Costs:

VAT rate: 12.5%
Returns: Quarterly (4 times/year)
Late filing penalty: ₹500 fixed
My fee (as local CA): ₹12,000/year
Software: Excel (₹0)
Total annual compliance cost: ~₹12,000

As % of revenue: 0.15%

Post-GST Era (2026 Reality after September 2025 Reforms):
Tax rate: 5% (without Input Tax Credit for most standalone restaurants)
Compliance Details (QRMP Scheme - Most Small Restaurants Opt In):

Eligible for turnover up to ₹5 crore: File GSTR-1 & GSTR-3B quarterly (4 times/year each) + annual GSTR-9
Monthly tax payment via PMT-06 challan
Total filings: ~9 per year (quarterly returns + annual + monthly payments)

Late Filing Penalties:

₹50/day CGST + ₹50/day SGST = ₹100/day total
Capped at ₹5,000–₹10,000 per return (depending on turnover/type)
Monthly delay example: ~₹3,000 (but capped quickly)

Professional Fees (2026 Rates):

CA fee: ₹30,000–₹50,000/year (increased due to reconciliation, notices)
GST software subscription: ₹10,000–₹20,000/year
Subtotal: ₹40,000–₹70,000

Input Tax Credit Reality:

Under 5% rate (standard for standalone non-AC restaurants): No ITC allowed
If opting for 18% rate (rare for small ones): ITC possible, but higher tax outflow
Mismatches/reconciliations still cause notices and extra time/cost: ~₹10,000–₹20,000/year in my experience

Real Total Annual Compliance Cost: ₹60,000–₹1,00,000/year
As % of ₹80L revenue: 0.75–1.25%
For a 10% profit margin business: This can eat 7.5–12.5% of profit.

The Cash Flow Challenge
Pre-GST: Pay tax quarterly
Post-GST: Monthly tax payments (even under QRMP)
Real Example:
Client gets ₹50,000 corporate order in late month → cheque delay → pay GST from pocket → cash flow strain. Happens often with corporate/B2B clients.

The Penalty & Notice Reality (Real Cases)
Case 1: Forgot one quarterly filing due to family emergency → penalty ~₹10,000–₹15,000 after cap + interest.
Case 2: Portal glitch/filed 1 day late → late fee applied, appeal possible but time-consuming.
Case 3: Vendor mismatch notice → extra time to fix, potential reversal if not resolved.
Many small businesses face stress from notices, even if errors are minor.

Why Some Restaurants Struggle or Close
From my clients (2023–2026):

High compliance time/cost relative to margins (several cases)
Penalties/notices adding up
Preference for composition scheme due to exhaustion

Composition Scheme Option:

Turnover up to ₹1.5 crore: Flat 5% on turnover, quarterly filing (simpler)
No ITC
For ₹80L turnover: Pay ₹4 lakhs GST (no credit) vs regular 5% with no ITC anyway → similar net tax, but simpler compliance
Many choose it to avoid detailed filings/reconciliations


The Numbers Mumbai Restaurants Face (2026)
Revenue: ₹80 lakhs/year
Gross Profit: ~₹8 lakhs (10%)
GST-Related Costs (Estimate):

CA fees: ₹40,000
Software: ₹15,000
Extra time/notices: ₹15,000–₹30,000
Total: ₹70,000–₹85,000

Net impact: Eats 9–11% of profit for many.

What Needs to Change (My Suggestions)

Raise exemption/threshold meaningfully (current ₹40L registration; suggest ₹1–2 crore for full relief).
Make quarterly filing default without monthly payments for <₹2Cr.
Better vendor ITC matching support.
Cap penalties more reasonably for genuine small errors.
Grace periods for new businesses.


What This Means for Mumbai's Economy
Small F&B closures hurt jobs and local wealth. Compliance burden, even simplified, hits hardest on micro businesses.
Large chains: Handle easily with teams/systems.
Small restaurants: Spend disproportionate time/energy on GST.
This wasn't the full promise of "Good and Simple Tax."

My Professional Opinion
The September 2025 reforms simplified slabs (mostly 5%/18%) and helped somewhat. But for tiny restaurants, compliance still feels heavy relative to size.
Solutions exist — political will needed.
