My daughter's Ira (4 years old) and I live in Bandra West, near the station.
Three months ago, we were walking to her playschool. A motorcycle came from behind—didn't see it, just the HORN.
Measured later on decibel app (similar bike): ~95 dB.
Ira jumped, fell, scraped her knee. Started crying. Shaking.
Since then:

Holds my hand tightly on footpaths
Asks "loud bike coming?" before going out
Covers ears for any vehicle
Wakes up multiple times at night
Refuses school some days

We're in therapy now. ₹3,000/session, twice a week.
Doctor diagnosed: Acute stress response from sudden loud noise.
The rider laughed and rode away.
This is my 4-year-old's life now. Because loud horns are common.

📊 The Decibel Reality (Mumbai Data)
Legal Limits:

Noise Pollution Rules: Residential day 55 dB
WHO: 55 dB safe continuous

Mumbai Levels (Recent Studies/Trends):

Average traffic: Often 75–85 dB
Horns: 90–100+ dB (modified higher)
Near stations/junctions: Frequently exceeds limits

Every 10 dB increase feels ~2x louder → 95 dB horn is much more intense.

🧠 Medical Impact on Children
Sudden >90 dB noise can trigger:

Cortisol spike/stress response
In kids: Anxiety, sleep issues, avoidance behaviors

Studies link chronic traffic noise (>70 dB) to higher anxiety/sleep problems in children.
What happened to Ira matches "phonophobia" or startle-induced anxiety patterns.
